## Implementation (APK, CI, tests)

We looked at whether your APK is functional and reliable. We also assessed your repo setup, whether it is clearly organized and if there is a CI running Sonar Cloud and running tests including at least one meaningful end-to-end test.

The APK is relatively stable and only crashed under the specific pattern demonstrated during the meeting. Input sanitization is generally well handled; however, the username, email, and password fields currently accept over 200 characters, which should be limited. This stability is expected since the app still relies on placeholders and lacks a backend connection. At this stage, there is no meaningful end-to-end user flow: while the onboarding is well implemented, the login process remains incomplete as signing in is not yet possible. Beyond navigation between empty pages, the app offers no real functionality and therefore does not meet the M1 requirements. Similarly, no end-to-end tests have been implemented.

As a team, you have a clear code review process documented in the GitHub wiki. It covers branch naming conventions, PR structure and descriptions, and the review workflow. You have also set regular standup meeting times and defined a time budget. Going forward, apply the PR structure and description rules consistently. Ensure each PR description provides sufficient detail about the changes introduced (this has generally been lacking). Continue following the branch naming conventions and aim for greater rigor in the upcoming sprints.

For this part, you received 4.13 points out of a maximum of 6.

## Sprint Backlog & Product Backlog

We assessed the structure and clarity of your Sprint and Product Backlogs and how you used them.
We looked at whether your tasks are well defined, appropriately sized, and aligned with user stories; whether the Product Backlog is well organized and value-driven; and whether the Sprint Backlog is continuously updated and demonstrates good planning and prioritization.

User stories and tasks are generally well structured: each task has one assignee, and user stories follow the correct template with size and priority defined. However, several improvements are needed. For the next sprint, ensure that parent relationships are added as instructed. The S1, S2, and S3 tags should no longer be used, as they provide no real value. Additionally, no estimated time has been set for tasks, and milestone M2 is missing. Some tasks also lack priority labels. Acceptance criteria should be clearly defined at the user story level, with each sub-sprint task addressing part of them (this is not currently the case). Finally, note that no user story has been moved to “Done” in S1 or S2, or the “User story” tag is missing from those items.

For this part, you received 4.5 points out of a maximum of 6.

## Figma

We evaluated your Figma, whether it is up-to-date with the app and how it is used as a communication, planning and design tool to accelerate the implementation of good UI.

Your Figma design is clean and consistent, reflecting a strong effort in planning the UI. It is slightly ahead of the current implementation in the APK, which is expected at this stage of development. The design closely mirrors what is already built in the app, and there is a clear distinction between wireframes and mockups. However, the overall structure of the Figma file does not follow user flows, which makes it harder to visualize end-to-end navigation. Additionally, the testing page has not yet been implemented; it would be helpful to include a section listing all test tags for better traceability.

For this part, you received 4.5 points out of a maximum of 6.

## Architecture

We reviewed your project’s architecture diagram, whether it is up-to-date with the app and if it follows clean design principles. We also looked at how it is used to discuss and document the architecture of the app as the app evolves.

The architecture diagram currently lacks alignment with the structure expected from the Android App Architecture guide outlined in the M1 deliverables. The diagram should clearly represent the separation between the UI Layer, Domain Layer, and Data Layer. At the moment, this separation is unclear. Also, UML diagrams are formal models that represent specific parts or aspects of a system, while architecture diagrams illustrate the high-level structure and organization of the entire system.

For this part, you received 3.5 points out of a maximum of 6.

## Scrum Process Documents

We reviewed your Scrum process documentation, including Sprint Retrospectives and Team Standup. We assessed whether they documented the process in detail, and how they were used to prepare the meetings. We also looked at your ability to lead structured and effective meetings, with everyone's participation.

Your Scrum process documentation is strong. With the exception of the S1 team retrospective, which was submitted late, all required Scrum documents were delivered on time and completed before their respective meetings.

As a team, you are all active and engaged during the meetings. There is a notable improvement in how you handle them; however, they are not yet highly structured or fully following the clear ceremonies process (without coaches having to intervene). Nonetheless, you consistently define a clear and detailed next sprint plan.

For this part, you received 5 points out of a maximum of 6.
